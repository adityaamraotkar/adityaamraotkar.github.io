Selected work

Supply and Demand Balancing workbook, Honeywell

Honeywell Process Technology is moving its supply planning from SAP APO SNP to Kinaxis. In the meantime, SNP could not show shortfalls and excess side by side, and it could not run scenarios, so planners kept their own local spreadsheets. As a Supply Planner Intern, I built an Advanced Planning System in Excel in 10 weeks to bridge that gap. The planning team now uses it to balance supply and demand across 34 production lines at 7 manufacturing sites in the US, Europe, and China.

  • Cascading dropdowns let planners filter by site, manufacturing line, procurement type, and profit center, then drill from base material down to SKU.
  • Each material gets a planning block with demand, supply, inventory, safety stock, deficit, and excess over a rolling 12 months.
  • A scenario override table lets planners change demand or supply for any month and compare versions without touching source data.
  • A four-tier heat map flags shortfall, below target, healthy, and excess against safety stock.
  • Global roll-up tabs sum every plant and line, at both SKU and base material level.
  • The calculation engine uses dynamic arrays (LET, FILTER, UNIQUE), XLOOKUP, INDEX/MATCH, SUMIFS, and COUNTIFS.
  • I pulled MRP data with SQL in Snowflake and Power BI, filtered 5,000+ materials down to 740+ active finished-goods SKUs, and joined BOM routing and work center tables to build a line-level material master.
  • I validated line, pack type, and procurement type assignments in SAP ECC (MD04, MD05) and worked through data discrepancies with the data science team.

I presented it to the project sponsors and leadership, then handed it off with a planner operating SOP and a Power BI build specification for the data pipeline.

Tools: Excel, Power Query, Power BI, SQL, Snowflake, SAP ECC, SAP APO (SNP). Details are kept general because the work is internal to Honeywell.

What I learned starting a company

Avert Automation

Avert offered preventative maintenance services to small and mid-sized manufacturers and industrial facilities around Charlotte. As co-founder I led B2B sales: prospecting, cold outreach, and on-site maintenance audits to find downtime risk for prospective clients. I also built the pitch deck and pricing, and spoke with angel investors. The company never reached revenue, and I have since stepped away. Here is what I took from it.

  • Know your number before you pitch. An investor asked how much I was raising and I did not have an answer yet.
  • Company structure affects fundraising. Our LLC did not work with standard SAFE paperwork.
  • Selling to busy facilities means removing steps. That is why technicians could log maintenance by scanning a QR code, with no login.
